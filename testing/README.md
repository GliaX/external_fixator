# Testing Parts Protocol

## Tightening Test
### Why to do it
The purpose of this test is to ensure that clamps can be tightened beyond what is expected intraoperatively without failure.

### How to do it
1. First test the bolt and nut by themselves to make sure that the threads are good and the nut can ride down the bolt without any problem. Use a regular nut, not a nylock nut.
2. Place a bolt through the rod clamp piece, with the bolt head in the hex trap.
3. Place a pin clamp piece onto the end of the bolt, and then secure both clamps with a washer and regular hex nut on the side of the pin clamp.
4. Secure bolted pieces in a vice.
5. Tighten the bolt with a torque wrench until failure. Measure failure point by incrementing torque wrench by 5-10 increments.

## Rod Test
### Why to do it
The purpose of this test is to ensure that the rod is straight, the Diameter is consistent, and is made of high grade stainless steel.

### How to do it
1. Visually inspect the rod. If there is any rust visible on stainless steel, this means that the layer of chromium oxide on the surface has been effected by mechanical wear, chemical corrosion, and/or contamination by iron particles, and therefore the rod is not suitable to use.
2. Use a magnet on the rod. If there is low magnetism, then the quality of stainless steel is adequate (316 or 304).
3. To test rod straightness, place the rod on a perfectly flat surface and then shine a light along the bottom where the rod is in contact with the surface. If any light shines through, the rod is not straight. 
4. Consistent Diameter can be tested with calipers.

## Tolerance Test
### Why to do it
The purpose of this test is to ensure that the tolerance of the holes in the clamps are good enough to allow the rods and pins to be inserted, and then tightened enough with the bolts and nuts so that the rods and pins do not move.

### How to do it
1. To check if holes are the proper sizes and tolerances, insert rod into the rod clamp hole, the pin into the pin clamp hole, and the bolt into the bolt hole of each printed clamp.
2. If the rods and pins do not fit into the holes of the respective clamps, a drill with a rod or pin used as the bit can be used to drill into the proper hole to widen it to the appropriate tolerance (test again after drilling).
3. The rod and pin should fit into the respective clamp with a bit of resistence, but not enough to have to use a lot of pressure to insert it.
4. If there is no resistence when inserting the rod and pins into their respective clamps, the tolerance of the holes might be too large. In this case, conduct a tightening test. If the rod or pin still moves after the tightening test, then the printed parts are not to be used.
5. If entry of the bolt into the bolt hole is not smooth, the bolt can be screwed in using a wrench. If this requires a lot of pressure, the tolerance of the bolt hole is too small and the part should not be used.
